# Personas

Two to four people who will use your system. Real roles, not demographics.

One paragraph each: who they are, what they are trying to get done, and what makes it hard
today. A persona you cannot picture doing their job is not useful.

You write these in Phase 2, from what your client told you — not from imagination.

---

## Eng. Mohammed, library administrator

Eng. Mohammed manages circulation inquiries and academic textbook reserves at the university library. He wants to ensure students have timely access to syllabus-approved course materials and monitor peer-to-peer textbook exchanges across campus through a moderation dashboard. Today, he has no visibility into student textbook trades, and students frequently come to the library circulation desk distressed after buying obsolete syllabus editions from unverified social media groups. He needs a straightforward interface to verify syllabus edition mappings and resolve flagged listings in between student visits without dealing with complex IT systems.

---

## Noura, student buyer

Noura is an undergraduate student registered for four courses this semester. She needs to secure clean, syllabus-approved textbooks before the drop-and-add period ends without spending 10 to 30 KWD per book on new bookstore copies. Today, she searches through unverified student WhatsApp and Telegram groups where listings omit edition numbers, sellers take days to respond or fail to show up, and she risks paying for obsolete editions that instructors reject in class. She will abandon any tool that forces her through long setup forms when she just needs to search a course code, verify the edition, and schedule a fast on-campus handoff between lectures.

---

## Fahad, student seller

Fahad is a senior student who finished his business courses with several clean, reusable textbooks sitting idle on his desk. He wants to list his finished books by course code and ISBN, state their condition, and sell or trade them to incoming students to recover costs and clear space. Today, posting in generic chat groups buries his messages under hundreds of class notes, attracts buyers who negotiate endlessly without committing, and leads to wasted trips across campus when buyers fail to meet at the agreed time. He needs a direct listing flow that matches his books against the active syllabus and locks in a clear campus meetup location.
## Eng. Mohammed, Library Administrator

Eng. Mohammed coordinates academic resource access and circulation services at the university library. He wants to ensure students can access approved course materials easily and safely on campus, while keeping an eye on textbook exchanges to prevent unapproved editions or counterfeit materials from circulating. Today, he has zero visibility into the textbook trades taking place across campus. When students arrive at the library help desk confused about syllabus requirements or complaining about scams from informal chat groups, library staff have no way to verify or intervene. He needs an administrative moderation dashboard that lets him review listings, verify that textbooks match active department syllabi, and monitor campus exchange volume without taking hours away from his daily library duties.

---

## Rashed, Student Seller

Rashed is a third-year student who finishes each semester with several clean, reusable textbooks sitting idle on his desk at home. He wants to list his completed course books by course code and ISBN, agree on a fair price or swap, and pass them on quickly to incoming students so he can recover some of his course expenses. Today, finding a buyer means posting across scattered, noisy WhatsApp and Telegram groups where his messages get lost in minutes. He wastes hours answering repetitive messages from non-serious inquiries, haggling over prices, and waiting on campus for buyers who frequently show up late or ghost the meetup entirely. He needs a dedicated platform where he can post a book once with its ISBN and condition rating, receive serious requests, and confirm a definite handoff at a secure campus location between his lectures.

---

## Fatima, Student Buyer

Fatima is a second-year undergraduate preparing for her new course schedule at the start of the semester. She wants to find clean, required textbooks for all her registered classes quickly and save roughly 50% compared to paying 10 to 30 KWD for brand-new bookstore copies. Today, searching for used copies is frustrating and risky: informal chat groups provide no guarantee of book condition, sellers take days to reply, and she constantly worries about payment scams or accidentally purchasing an outdated edition that lacks the current problem sets required by her professor. She needs to search directly by course code, see instant verification that a listing matches her active syllabus edition, and schedule a safe in-person handoff right at the campus library.
